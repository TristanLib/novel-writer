# Historical and transmigration variant

Read this reference only when the story depends on historical institutions,
examinations, bureaucracy, guilds, transplanted knowledge, identity
replacement, or borrowed famous texts.

## Relative low position without reset

Let a new city, academy, guild, ministry, court, or market make the protagonist
comparatively small without deleting prior gains. Reputation may arrive before
the person, allies may be distant, documents may grant review rather than
acceptance, and local rules may price old strengths differently.

## Knowledge advantage boundary

Separate:

1. recognizing a pattern;
2. explaining it in locally legible language;
3. accessing materials and records;
4. gaining standing to propose action;
5. creating authority and incentives for adoption;
6. bearing unintended cost and counter-strategy.

A transplanted protagonist often owns only the first two early. Earn the
remaining steps through people, resources, institutions, trial, and cost.

## Institution-first conflict

Use locally legible objects and practices: registers, seals, copies, witnesses,
guarantees, contracts, assessment rubrics, lodging rules, delivery chains,
notices, prices, and custody.

Adapt new rules from existing practice. Show:

- who performs extra work;
- who pays for paper, time, storage, or enforcement;
- which privacy, patronage, or livelihood interest is threatened;
- what limited pilot or bargain makes adoption plausible;
- what failure condition stops the rule.

Avoid dropping modern legal, technical, or management vocabulary directly into
historical dialogue.

## Time and qualification spine

When an arc crosses months or ends in examination, appointment, promotion,
guild admission, or legal status change, track:

- entry route for every major candidate;
- recurring coursework, service, attendance, or assessment;
- missed work and repair or disqualification;
- lodging, income, illness, and duties across seasonal jumps;
- exact authority that certifies final eligibility.

Inherited status or a patron's seat must not silently become a passing score.
Do not let final qualification rest on `a year passed` without visible work.

## Historical actors remain intelligent

Officials, merchants, scholars, performers, servants, craftspeople, and
patrons know their own world better than the protagonist. Give local experts
information, craft, incentives, and counterarguments the protagonist lacks.

## Identity and memory boundaries

Track what the protagonist remembers, what outside characters observed, and
what each person believes about changed behavior. Outside characters must not
speak as if they know about transmigration, possession, or missing memories
without on-page evidence.

## Borrowed-text pressure

Treat a famous borrowed text as both asset and evidence. Track:

- exact wording and variants;
- physical or oral carrier;
- attribution and first witness;
- circulation and profit;
- style, chronology, and source questions;
- what the protagonist can honestly claim;
- who is harmed when authorship or version control fails.

Every major borrowed-text win should deepen a future consistency burden.
Balance it with judgments, choices, work, and costs the protagonist can truly
own.

## Mystery and access discipline

Do not let an outline grant access to sealed archives, powerful patrons,
dangerous sites, or final truth without earned conditions. A local payoff may
prove that a document moved, a procedure was manipulated, or a rumor has a real
source while leaving the ultimate author, order, or motive unresolved.

## Research triggers

Verify period-specific institutions, titles, calendars, travel time, document
forms, markets, medicine, law, material culture, and performance settings when
accuracy matters. If the world intentionally blends periods, define and follow
its internal rule instead of claiming strict history.
