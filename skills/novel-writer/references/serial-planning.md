# Serial planning

Use this reference for volume, arc, stage, and multi-chapter structure. Do not
use it as a substitute for chapter prose, formal continuity acceptance, or
state synchronization.

## Contents

- Planning units
- Workflow: authority, advice, start state, contract, promises, stages, cards,
  public payoffs, retention, and validation
- Hard rejections

## Planning units

- **Chapter = reading transaction.** Attention must buy a result, answer,
  emotional turn, relationship movement, irreversible choice, recognition, or
  visible cost.
- **Stage = escalation and dividend unit.** Answer one local question, change
  leverage, and make the next problem necessary.
- **Volume = promise and payoff unit.** Give the opening core question a visible
  answer even when larger mysteries continue.

## Workflow

### 1. Load project authority

Inspect project instructions, current progress, series and character rules,
style guidance, the previous volume's closing state, relevant evidence or
domain ledgers, and existing outline conventions.

Use completed prose as the strongest story evidence. Treat external market
research as an idea source, not authority over the manuscript.

### 2. Audit outside advice

Classify every imported technique as:

- **Adopt**: use as written because it fits the project and evidence;
- **Adapt**: keep the mechanism but change cadence, scale, or boundary;
- **Reject**: document why it conflicts with canon, genre promise, evidence, or
  reader experience.

Treat unsupported statistics, inaccessible citations, platform folklore, and
rigid ratios as hypotheses. Never write them into story canon.

### 3. Freeze the start state

Record:

- identity and formal status;
- money, lodging, tools, documents, and usable authority;
- relationships, obligations, injuries, debts, and promises;
- object and evidence custody plus knowledge boundaries;
- important text versions, attribution, circulation, and source risk;
- unresolved threads and truths forbidden to reveal in this volume.

A new arena may lower the protagonist's relative status, but it must not erase
earned skill, reputation, allies, resources, or consequences.

For long spans or institutionally earned outcomes, also map recurring work,
attendance, assessment, income, lodging, injuries, eligibility, and seasonal
time jumps. Do not let a final qualification rest on work that exists only in a
summary.

### 4. Write the volume contract

Define:

- reader promise and core question;
- visible public problem;
- protagonist's concrete goal and inner pressure;
- scarce capability plus limit and cost;
- opposing order logic: what it protects, who benefits, what it fears, its red
  line, and compromise condition;
- opening state, irreversible end state, price of victory, and next-volume
  door;
- truths, rewards, and shortcuts unavailable in this volume.

If the volume ending cannot visibly answer the core question, revise the
contract before expanding chapter cards.

### 5. Build the promise/payoff map

Use stable IDs such as `V02-P01`. Record:

- first plant;
- reader expectation;
- intermediate dividends;
- final payoff window;
- pressure created by payoff;
- final state: `closed`, `transformed`, or `carry-forward`.

Give every carry-forward promise a fair reason and a new valid window. Payoff
does not require total revelation: a local answer, changed status, relationship
decision, resource loss, or proven limit can pay a promise while a larger
mystery continues.

### 6. Design stages before chapters

Give every stage:

- entry condition and visible objective;
- central conflict and opposing high ground;
- action chain rather than a list of information drops;
- public or externally verifiable scene when appropriate;
- stage dividend and irreversible change;
- cost, backlash, or wider attention;
- exit condition that makes the next stage necessary.

Escalate by enlarging the problem, affected interests, and required authority,
not only by introducing a stronger antagonist.

### 7. Expand chapter cards stage by stage

Make each card show:

- immediate goal or question;
- one-sentence ordinary-reader contract;
- resistance or legitimate opposing high ground;
- central action and meaningful choice;
- immediate consequence of that choice;
- opening pressure, deadline, desire, or cost;
- promised same-chapter payoff and latest delivery point;
- visible reading-state change;
- scene-level genre or premise payoff;
- specific reason to continue;
- hook type compared with the previous three cards;
- explanation, recap, or procedure budget;
- cost, unavailable shortcut, and capability boundary;
- promise IDs advanced;
- concrete handoff;
- `cannot write` boundary for sensitive canon or evidence.

Do not pre-write dialogue to hide missing causality. A card must be specific
enough to produce a scene, choice, result, and handoff.

### 8. Design public payoffs under observable rules

Identify:

- public space and public problem;
- who has standing to decide;
- the opposing side's defensible rule;
- witnesses and verifiable objects or texts;
- turning hinge;
- visible result;
- immediate benefit and lasting price.

Prefer winning by the stated rule and exposing its hidden asymmetry over sudden
crowd-wide admiration. Let reasonable observers disagree for concrete reasons.

### 9. Run the planning retention gate

Read `retention-audit.md`. Give each chapter card a provisional four-sentence
recall and evidence-backed eight-dimension score. Cite the planned on-page
scene, choice, result, and handoff. Revise cards that require outline
explanation to make their payoff or next drive visible.

At every 3-, 5-, and 10-chapter boundary, inspect setup runs, return diversity,
hook repetition, irreversible change, promise dividends, and stage results.
Use windows as diagnostics, not win quotas: failure, sacrifice, lost leverage,
or a binding choice can provide the correct reader dividend.

Replace provisional plan scores with prose-based scores after drafting.

### 10. Validate and hand off

Run `scripts/audit_serial_outline.py` when the outline uses compatible Markdown
tables. Treat warnings as prompts for judgment; the script never evaluates
literary quality.

Remove unresolved alternatives from irreversible outcomes before freezing the
plan. Exact ranks, deciding questions, custody transfers, authority, and final
status cannot remain as `A or B` placeholders.

Then run formal continuity review and synchronize project state. If review
changes the plan, rerun structural validation.

## Hard rejections

- Do not impose a win every three chapters or a public triumph every ten.
- Do not reset growth through arbitrary amnesia, bankruptcy, lost competence,
  or severed relationships.
- Do not make local experts collectively foolish so the protagonist appears
  clever.
- Do not solve every stage with a stronger patron, famous text, hidden power,
  or identical humiliation scene.
- Do not close a long mystery merely because a cadence ratio says payoff is due.
- Do not generate a hundred chapter cards from one copied template.
